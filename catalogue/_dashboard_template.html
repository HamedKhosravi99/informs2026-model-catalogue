A member's value is not its score

The forecast is two halves. Eleven diverse models share one half — the bracket. A single model holds the other half by itself: the anchor, the same bi-directional GRU as two bracket members but averaged over 100 networks (25 seeds × 4 snapshots), which makes it the most accurate and steadiest single object we have. The bracket supplies disagreement; the anchor holds the forecast still.

Below, each object sits by how good it is alone (horizontal) against how much its errors resemble the bracket (vertical). The useful direction is down, not left. The three stock-flow models are the weakest in the set and the least correlated — and no substitute survived a replacement test. Hover any point.

Every selection procedure we tried reversed

Each line is one way of choosing something from the data — weights, a member subset, a gate, a threshold. The left end is what it scored when the choice was made and judged on the same folds; the right end is the same procedure with the choice itself moved inside the cross-validation. Every line goes the wrong way. This is why the shipped model contains no member chosen by cross-validation rank.

Build your own ensemble

Tick any set of the 60 models below and see what an equal-weight average of them actually scores — including the pretrained foundation models, the two-stage and hurdle formulations, and every modern architecture we tried. The numbers are exact, not interpolated: they come from the models' error cross-products, so each result is what you would have measured by running that combination. Try adding the best models one at a time and watch it stop helping. Two notes on what is and is not here. Chronos-2 is the only pretrained model that produced predictions — TabPFN, TabDPT, TabICLv2, Mitra and SAP RPT-1 were all prepared as drop-in learners but never ran, blocked by a licence gate, a GPU requirement, or stopped for cost, so there is nothing to score. And the modern architectures below are not foundation models: PatchTST, DLinear, iTransformer, TimeXer, XLinear and TSMixer come from that literature but we define and fit each one here on the 239 training counties — the only from_pretrained call anywhere in the codebase loads Chronos-2. Conformal prediction is in the catalogue but cannot be a member at all: it changes the interval, not the point forecast.
